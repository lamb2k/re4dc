## Ordinary DataCtrl read ownership

`datactrl.cpp:239..389` allocates a fresh unit-owned destination in condition0, or uses an explicit/fixed buffer, before DvdReadN. `include/datactrl.h` stores wait as u8; `wait|0x10` cannot introduce mode0x100. These requests are ordinary queue work, dispatched to task3, not the native ISR path.

`datactrl.cpp:561..598` setClear condition1/3 calls ReadCancel(mode0x40), makes ONE ReadCheck, then checkMallocRelease. It has no local wait loop or queue-wide lock. It is therefore incorrect to describe the function itself as a generic synchronous drain. Nevertheless the current ordinary scheduler parks task3 between whole Read steps: `scheduler.cpp:156..236` waits nativeTaskYielded, and TaskSleep331..363 requires a resume token. KOS filesystem yielding is not an ordinary source task yield. A source task cannot simply free the destination concurrently with an ordinary worker's guarded executing step under this dispatch protocol.

`dvd.cpp:1021..1061` brackets each whole step with native DVD/IO scope; `readProcMain:1485..1496` sleeps between steps for non-ISR requests. `readMain:783..989` reads through DVD_BUFF and tests cancellation before the later staging-to-destination transfer. A pending single-clear request can finish cancellation after the unit frees its buffer; the ordinary parked-step and cancel check are the relevant preconditions preventing a later payload transfer. Neither a single poll nor the platform cancellation callback independently proves this.

`DC.initDataUnit:875..897` byte-clears all descriptors and does not itself cancel ordinary DVD requests or free their owned allocations. This loses ordinary per-unit metadata, but the normal door's later cancellation precedes heap reuse; whole-room heap ownership may intentionally reclaim those allocations. A leak/UAF claim needs an actual allocation surviving/reused outside that boundary, not just the missing per-unit free. Ordinary unit commands and DVD IDs have no allocation generation token; queue identity is an 8-bit nonzero ID, distinct from a buffer epoch. Reuse/ID-wrap adversarial sequences were not promoted to normal reachability.
