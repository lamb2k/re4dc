# RE4 Dreamcast retrospective, 2026-11-19 to 11-20

Covers the two days from the self-assessment to the stop: about 15 lanes, 11 hand-overs (H-001 to H-011), three independent audits. Tiers: **console** (real Dreamcast), **MODELLED** (PC hardware model), **Flycast**.

Good progress on the engine. No proof on hardware, and nothing a player can use yet.

## What worked

1. **Measuring before planning.** The console profile split into budget rows showed where the time is: the original decision code is under 1 ms a tick, and publication plus services are 32 of 55 ms. Every later choice followed from that table.
2. **A separate engine with no publication layer.** The publication row went from 16.3 ms to about zero by design. The audit still finds small conversions, but the big cost is gone.
3. **The bench.** Once the profile named the causes (library math calls, lost kernels, per-piece overhead), one lane took drawing from 45 to 14 ms MODELLED.
4. **Native services proven on the PC.** The PC reference build (re4ref) let skeleton, collision, cloth and animation keys be proven equal to the original in hours, with no emulator. Services fell by more than half.
5. **Independent audits.** Three audits caught three real overclaims: round 2's 52% that was 18 to 30%, the E0 clipping bug and unscalable visibility table, and E1's retained original code. Without them we would be building on wrong numbers.
6. **Briefs with failing controls.** Every check shipped with an input that must fail. Lanes delivered in one to three hours each, and their numbers held up when re-derived.
7. **Asking outside.** The F-Zero session's answer ("the gain came from not hosting the hot systems") and the Dreamcast survey (compiler flags, library calls in hot loops) both turned straight into gains.
8. **Fast owner decisions.** Phone-sized still sheets and one-question forks got answers in minutes.

## What didn't

1. **No console run.** Two days of numbers, none from hardware. The plan said one model round per milestone, then the console. With the SD card away, we ran more model rounds instead of switching to work that doesn't depend on timing.
2. **Too much in flight.** About 15 lanes in a day. The integrator merged, fixed link breaks and resolved conflicts instead of checking work. Seven discs were staged; several went stale within hours.
3. **"For now" became permanent.** Hosting lanes linked original service code to get characters running. Nothing checked the link, so the rule against it wasn't enforced until the audit.
4. **Reported before audited.** "Done" went to the owner on lane summaries. Two of those were then cut by audits.
5. **The exactness trap, again.** Tick-for-tick matching with the old game grew into fixtures, feeds, a reference build, then a split reference. Rules changed under running lanes three times (keep the random order, split it, aim sway back).
6. **Brief mistakes.** Four of the first five lane stops came from the integrator's briefs: a wrong reference hash, a folder rule with nobody assigned to it, an unclear stop rule, an undefined baseline.
7. **Scope grew into running lanes.** Whole game, Ashley, bosses: each became a lane the same hour instead of a line in a backlog.
8. **The cheap levers didn't move drawing.** Content steps saved 0.5 ms. The hill, with two characters, is already at the floor on the model (30.5 ms mean). Drawing is over its row in every scene.
9. **The tool that unblocks the console kept slipping.** The remote loader's proof run was bumped four times for bigger lanes.

## Keep doing

- Budget rows as the scoreboard, in absolute ms, with the tier on every number.
- The bench as the permanent drawing benchmark.
- PC-first proof for services, with failing controls.
- Independent audit per milestone, and before any number is used to decide.
- One-question forks to the owner, with stills.
- Hosting original behaviour (it is nearly free) and replacing only what is under it.

## Stop doing

- Calling anything done before its audit and its console run.
- More than two lanes, or more than one milestone, at a time.
- Linking original service code to get a demo running.
- Tick-exact matching with the old game.
- Starting a lane the hour a new idea arrives.
- Model-only tuning when the console is unavailable.
- Staging a disc per lane.

## Start doing

- **A console run at every milestone, booked before the milestone starts.** If the console is unavailable, switch to work whose value does not depend on timing (hosting game systems, tools), not more speed rounds.
- **A link check in the merge gate** that rejects original service symbols.
- **A backlog** in the status board. New scope goes there, and the integrator proposes where it fits.
- **"Finished, unaudited"** as the only status a lane can report.
- **One evening disc** for the console, rebuilt from the integration branch, instead of a disc per lane.
- **Plays right by outcome:** a scripted player on both builds, comparing kills, damage, waves, triggers and time to clear.
- **Integrator checks before merging:** rules, link, and the lane's own controls re-run, not the lane's summary.
